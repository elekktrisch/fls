AlpenFlight — three design directions

The same two screens in three visual personalities. Hero screen: the flight form on a phone (long scroll, sticky summary, sticky Save). Secondary: the logbook on a desktop, with four flight states visible. The content is real: Birrfeld LSZF, ASK 21 HB-3215, tow HB-EAB. Pick one, mix two, or reject all three.

Direction A

Instrument

A glass cockpit. Dark ground, one cyan for what is live, one amber for what is locked. Every number is monospaced and column-aligned, so a wrong digit shows itself. Nothing is decorative. The screen looks like avionics because it does the same job.

Direction B

Timetable

A Swiss printed document. White ground, hard black rules, Helvetica-class type, and no shadow anywhere. One red, used only for what is unfinished or wrong. The screen reads like an SBB departure board: dense, factual, and hard to misread.

Direction C

Tool

A Sinn watch or a Leica body. Warm off-white ground, graphite chrome, hairline rules, and exactly one red that appears once per screen. The restraint carries the reliability: the screen stays quiet, so the one item that needs attention gets it.
