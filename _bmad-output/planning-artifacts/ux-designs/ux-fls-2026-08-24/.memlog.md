- (decision) Create-mode UX run. Sources confirmed: product brief + addendum (brief-fls-2026-08-24). Reference input: docs/attempt-1/design-reference/ — vendored Claude Design bundle from 2026-05-21, treated as history under question, not as authority.
- (decision) Operator states the target feel: Swiss exactness, aero, reliability. The UI must feel like an instrument, not like a web app.
- (decision) Working mode: coaching path. Creative tools woven into discovery.
- (decision) Flight form shape: ONE dense form, no wizard. Overrides the attempt-1 three-step wizard (docs/attempt-1/design-reference/screens-entry.jsx). Rationale: brief names click-and-keystroke parity with legacy FLS as the second product advantage; a wizard adds two clicks minimum. Fields hide by aircraft and start type, not by step.
- (decision) Stakes: internal. Club members and staff only. Pragmatic accessibility floor, not a consumer or regulated floor. NOTE: the brief still describes public trial-flight and passenger-flight flows (screens-public.jsx in the reference) - confirm whether those public surfaces are in scope for this UX run.
- (change) Operator refines the flight-form decision: the form must also be efficient on mobile, and one single form may not be correct on a phone. The one-dense-form decision therefore applies to the desktop variant. The mobile variant is open and needs its own shape. Constraint context: brief names the phone as the daily hot path (operator at the airfield, poor coverage); rebuild-1 C21/C22 called for mobile-first plus a dense desktop variant.
- (decision) Mobile flight form: long scroll, one column, sticky summary header, sticky Save. No accordion, no stepper, no More-fields control. The sticky header shows the flight identity so far (aircraft, take-off time).
- (decision) Draft rule: autosave every field to local storage as typed, PLUS an explicit draft list on the logbook screen. The operator can hold several part-entered flights during a busy day. No Save-draft button. This serves the offline requirement and the constant-interruption reality.
- (assumption) The 'no hiding' in the chosen mobile option means no accordion collapsing. It does NOT cancel legacy conditional field visibility: the form still hides fields that the selected aircraft and start type make irrelevant (88 conditional directives in the three legacy flight-edit templates). Needs operator confirmation.
- (event) Rendered three design directions to .working/directions-3.html. A=Instrument (dark glass cockpit, cyan live / amber locked, mono numbers). B=Timetable (white, hard black rules, Helvetica-class, one red). C=Tool (warm off-white, graphite chrome, one signal red per screen). Each shows the mobile flight form plus the desktop logbook with four flight states.
- (decision) Conditional field visibility CONFIRMED as parity behaviour. The mobile 'no hiding' choice refers only to the accordion. Fields still show and hide according to the selection in other fields (winch launch shows no tow plane and no release altitude). Source: 88 conditional directives across the three legacy flight-edit templates, Addendum section 2.
- (decision) Visual direction: A - INSTRUMENT. Glass-cockpit register. Dark ground, cyan for live, amber for locked, monospaced tabular numbers, radius 0, no decoration. Chosen over B Timetable and C Tool. Operator accepted the sunlight and battery caution raised against a dark ground.
- (decision) What feels right in legacy FLS: the flight-log entry form. Named strengths: in-form master data creation, time pickers, dropdowns - many in-form capabilities that keep the operator on one screen. This is the behaviour to reproduce, not the appearance.
- (decision) Search is a NAMED WEAKNESS of legacy FLS. Per-column filter and sort seemed straightforward but is inefficient in use. The operator wants a Google-style single search field, combined with a small set of deliberate filters. This is a departure from legacy behaviour and it needs a recorded decision, because the brief forbids unrecorded behaviour change.
- (decision) The accounting-rules screens are hard to understand in the legacy UI. This is a named design opportunity, not only a port. It aligns with the brief: the buyer persona is the club system carrier, and their true objection is the risk that billing changes. A comprehensible rules UI answers that objection directly.
- (decision) Trustworthiness definition, in the operator's words: instant reaction to every click; data reliably saved, always; no playfulness; everything straightened and exact; no misaligned elements; no flickering during loading. This drives the State Patterns and Interaction Primitives sections: no layout shift, no spinner flash, reserved space for loading, strict alignment, one motion duration, no decorative animation.
- (decision) Mode policy: DARK ONLY. One ground, no light mode, no setting, no branch. The operator accepted the sunlight and battery cost after the caution was raised twice. CONSEQUENCE for DESIGN.md: the dark palette must carry an unusually high contrast floor so it survives direct sunlight. Target the WCAG AAA body-text ratio, not the AA minimum, and forbid low-contrast greys for any data value.
- (decision) Logbook search model: ONE search field plus filter chips. The field matches across aircraft, pilot, date, and remarks at once. Chips below it narrow by date range, aircraft, pilot, and state. Column headers keep SORT and LOSE per-column filtering. This replaces the legacy per-column filter and sort model.
- (change) Spinner rule refined by the operator. A spinner IS allowed, but only for a genuinely heavy operation - for example an invoice run, a migration import, or a large export. A spinner is NOT allowed for normal CRUD, for navigation, or for a list load. Those must use prefetch, optimistic write, and reserved space. This replaces the earlier absolute 'no spinner' floor.
- (decision) Root cause of the accounting-rules comprehension problem, found in legacy source (flsweb/src/masterdata/accountingRules/). A rule is a WHEN/THEN pair plus an engine-stop flag, but the 567-line edit template flattens all three into one undifferentiated field list, and the list template sorts only by name, description, target and type. The engine is a decrement loop, so RULE ORDER decides the invoice - and the UI never displays order. WHEN fields: rule filter type, aircraft, start and landing locations, start types, flight types, crew types, person category, member state, club members, home base, flight-duration band. THEN fields: article, accounting unit type, recipient, delivery line text, three no-landing-tax flags, charged-to-club-internal. Engine control: STOP_RULE_ENGINE_WHEN_RULE_APPLIED. Cross-reference: addendum section 7 records rule order as open question Q-B6.
- (decision) Accounting rules UI: ordered list plus WHEN/THEN rule cards plus a dry run. The list shows rules in engine run order and allows reorder. Each rule reads as a WHEN/THEN sentence with the engine-stop flag visible. The dry run takes one real flight and shows which rules fire, in order, what each consumes from the remaining time, and what each emits. The engine behaviour does not change; only the screen changes.
- (decision) Reorder guard: show the effect before it commits. A reorder runs the dry run against a recent real flight and shows the invoice before and after, side by side. The carrier confirms with the numbers visible. This depends on the dry run.
- (decision) The dry run serves a second purpose: migration verification. Brief step 3 requires the club to inspect its data before it commits, including the accounting rules. A dry run that replays a recorded legacy flight and reproduces the same invoice lines to the cent is that proof. One surface, two jobs. Record this for bmad-prd and bmad-architecture.
- (decision) Public no-auth surfaces (trial-flight registration, passenger-flight registration, landing page) are IN SCOPE and carry a STRICTER floor than the signed-in app: a consumer accessibility floor and consumer wording, because a stranger meets them on an unknown device. The signed-in app keeps the internal floor. Two registers in one product. This qualifies the earlier stakes=internal answer.
- (decision) Top-level navigation: OPERATE / PLAN / RECORDS / ADMIN. Four destinations named by job, not by entity. Operate = log flight and the logbook. Plan = reservations, the scheduler, and planning days. Records = members, aircraft, and reports. Admin = master data, accounting rules, users, deliveries, and system. Phone shows four bottom tabs. This replaces both the legacy domain menu and the attempt-1 Home/Logbook/Reservations/Aircraft/Members shape.
- (decision) IA source of truth: docs/modernization/01-current-state.md section 2 feature inventory. Eight domains: identity/auth/tenancy; master data; flight operations; reservations and planning; accounting and invoicing; public no-auth flows; email and scheduled jobs; dashboard.
- (decision) JOURNEY (operator narration, 2026-08-25). The flying day starts before the day. 1) Season start: someone assigns the operator and the tow pilot for every day of every weekend, in one batch, with corrections later. 2) Over the following weeks, glider pilots reserve aircraft for timeslots. 3) On the day, the operator explains the plan to the glider pilots. 4) When the first glider pilot sits at the runway start, the operator enters BOTH aircraft (tow and glider) and BOTH pilots - before the flight happens. 5) When the gear leaves the ground, the operator presses a dedicated NOW button to set the block start. 6) After the landing, EITHER the operator on a laptop OR the pilots themselves on a mobile record the landing time. 7) On a training flight the second crew member is the instructor, who adds notes before the next flight starts, or later. 8) At some point the flight logs lock; the operator wants the legacy timing kept.
- (change) CORRECTION to the draft decision (memlog entry 8). The narration shows a partly filled flight is NOT a private draft. It is an OPEN FLIGHT that other people finish on other devices: the operator starts it on a laptop at the runway, and a pilot completes the landing time on a phone. A local-storage draft is private to one device and would break that handover. REVISED MODEL: a started flight is a server-side record in an open state, visible to every user in the club. Local autosave stays, but only as the OFFLINE BUFFER for a device that cannot reach the server. The named draft list becomes a list of open flights, which is shared, not private.
- (decision) The NOW button is load-bearing. Legacy FLS has a dedicated NOW control that stamps the block start at the moment the gear leaves the ground. It is the most pressed control of a flying day, it is pressed while watching an aircraft, and it must be one tap with no confirmation, large, and reachable by a thumb. Future intent recorded by the operator: derive the block start automatically from FLARM vectors. Design the control so an automatic source can replace the tap without a screen change.
- (decision) A flight record is written by several people, on several devices, over several hours. Entry points: operator at the runway (aircraft and crew), operator at wheels-up (block start), operator on a laptop OR pilot on a phone (landing time), instructor (notes, later). The interaction model is therefore partial entry, save, reopen by somebody else. The one-dense-form decision governs the SHAPE of the form. It does not describe the dominant interaction.
- (decision) New surface required, not present in the attempt-1 reference: an AIRBORNE board. The operator must see which flights are in the air now - block start set, no landing time - and press NOW or enter a landing against any of them. This is the operator home screen during a flying day, and it is distinct from the logbook.
- (decision) Planning assignment is a BATCH task, not a per-day task. Someone assigns the operator and the tow pilot across every weekend day of a whole season at once, then corrects single days later. The planning surface must support bulk assignment across a date range first, and single-day correction second.
- (decision) Instructor notes arrive LATE and out of order. The instructor adds notes to a finished flight before the next flight starts, or hours later. There must be a fast path from anywhere to add a note to a recent flight, without navigating the full logbook.
- (assumption) Flight locking keeps the legacy timing. The operator does not know the exact rule. Addendum section 7 records it as open: a flight locks after two days and bills three days later, and the unit and the boundary are undocumented (Q-B2, Q-B3). UX requirement independent of the answer: the lock must be visible and predictable. A flight must show when it will lock, so the deadline never surprises the instructor who adds notes late.
- (decision) Concurrency model: ONE HOLDER AT A TIME. The person who opens an open flight holds it. Everybody else sees it read-only with the holder name and a Take over control.
- (decision) CONSEQUENCE 1 of the holder model - the hold must expire. The operator opens a flight on a laptop at the runway and walks away. Without a timeout the pilot is blocked from entering a landing time. The hold therefore releases automatically after a short idle period, and it releases when the holder closes the flight. Take over always stays available and always names who is being taken over from.
- (decision) CONSEQUENCE 2 of the holder model - stamp actions bypass the hold. NOW (block start) and Landing are single-field, atomic stamps pressed from the airborne board. They do NOT take the hold and they are never blocked by one. Otherwise the operator cannot stamp a block start on a flight a pilot is holding, which is the exact moment the stamp matters. Only the full edit form takes the hold.
- (assumption) CONSEQUENCE 3 of the holder model - offline. A device with no network cannot acquire or release a hold. Assumed rule: an offline device queues its edits locally and marks them unsent; on reconnect it applies them if no conflicting change arrived, and shows a conflict for the operator to resolve if one did. An offline device never blocks anybody else. Needs operator confirmation.
- (decision) Operator home screen: a DASHBOARD, with the airborne board as one panel on it. Other panels: today, reservations, and expiring licences and medicals. The airborne panel carries the NOW and Landing controls directly.
- (change) List treatment changed by the operator. Lists use STRIPS (alternating tone bands) instead of a hairline rule under every row. Applies to every list of records: logbook, airborne board, deliveries, members, aircraft, reports. New token surface-band #0E141B sits between surface-base and surface-panel. A row keeps its full height, so the bands stay even and the alignment stays visible. Rule under every row is removed; the header keeps its line-strong rule below it. OPEN: whether form field rows follow the same strip treatment or keep hairlines.
- (change) CORRECTION - the facilitator misread the strip request. The first attempt applied zebra banding to a table of equal cells. The operator means a COMPOSED RECORD STRIP, like the rows on the mobile dashboard: each field gets its own formatting according to its importance. A strip has an identity zone (the aircraft registration, in value-lead mono), a meta zone (crew and times, in micro secondary), a metric zone (the leading number, in value mono, right aligned), a state marker below the metric, and an optional action at the far right. This replaces the data table on EVERY list, on the phone AND on the pointer device. Both readings of the original request are kept: the rows are composed strips, and they are separated by the alternating band rather than by a rule under every row.
- (decision) CONSEQUENCE of the record strip - sorting moves. A strip has no columns, so a column header cannot carry the sort. The sort control moves into the list toolbar, beside the search field and the filter chips. Toolbar order: search field, then filter chips, then the sort control.
- (decision) The record strip needs FIXED ZONES so the values still line up down the list. The identity zone has a fixed width. The metric zone has a fixed width and is right aligned. The meta zone takes the remaining space. This keeps the operator requirement that nothing is misaligned, without a table.
- (decision) Dense mode on a pointer device collapses the record strip from two lines to one. The identity, the meta, and the metric sit on one line, and the state marker moves to the far right. The zones keep their fixed widths, so the alignment survives the change.
- (change) The alternating band is REMOVED. The operator does not want it. A record strip has one background. Rows are separated by their own composition and by the group headers, not by a band and not by a rule under every row.
- (change) The record strip has TWO LAYOUTS, not one. The three-zone side-by-side layout is the POINTER-DEVICE layout only. Forcing it onto a 330px phone squeezed the meta zone and wrapped the text. PHONE LAYOUT: the strip stacks. A left block takes the full remaining width and holds the identity on line 1 and the meta on line 2. A right block holds the metric on line 1 and the state marker on line 2, right aligned and sized to its content. The action slot (NOW, LAND) sits at the far right. The meta therefore gets almost the whole width and does not wrap.
- (decision) The fixed-zone alignment rule now applies to the POINTER-DEVICE layout only. On a phone the alignment comes from the two stacked blocks: every identity starts at the same left edge, and every metric ends at the same right edge. Nothing is misaligned, and no width is wasted.
